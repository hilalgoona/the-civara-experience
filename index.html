01 / THE YARN

Hand-spun and machine-spun Pashmina

Hand-spun yarn is drawn and twisted by a person using a traditional spinning tool. The process is skill-intensive, and the yarn may show natural variation in its character.

Machine-spun yarn is produced using spinning machinery, which can create more regular and consistent yarn. Machine spinning does not, by itself, tell us whether the fibre is genuine Pashmina.

Fibre composition, spinning method and weaving method are different facts. A responsible description states each one accurately and uses appropriate evidence for material claims.

02 / PATTERN THROUGH WEAVING

Kani and Jamawar

In traditional Kani weaving, the weaver uses small wooden bobbins called kanis to introduce coloured weft threads and build a pattern as the cloth is woven. Complex designs demand planning, patience and considerable skill.

Jamawar describes a rich patterned textile tradition. Kani weaving is one important way elaborate Jamawar designs may be made; the terms are related, but they are not simply interchangeable.

03 / PATTERN THROUGH NEEDLEWORK

Sozni embroidery

Sozni is a fine Kashmiri hand-embroidery tradition. An artisan uses a needle and thread to build detailed motifs stitch by stitch, often following a design placed on the textile. The fineness and coverage of the embroidery can require long hours of skilled work.

Unlike Kani, which builds a pattern during weaving, Sozni adds decoration through embroidery after the base textile has been woven.
